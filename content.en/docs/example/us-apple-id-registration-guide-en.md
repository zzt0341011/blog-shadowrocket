Plenty of apps that can't be found on the mainland China App Store become available
once you sign in with a US account. But before you can use US resources, the first
move isn't jumping straight into the "create account" page — it's changing your
iPhone's system region to the United States first, because the Apple account
registration flow follows whatever region the system is currently set to. If the
region is wrong, the account you end up registering will have the wrong region too.
[Here's the process laid out step by step](https://shadowrocket.ink).

## Step 1: Open Settings

Find the "Settings" icon on your home screen and tap it.

![Open iPhone Settings](https://shadowrocket.ink/img/id-10001.jpg)

## Step 2: Switch the system region to the United States

Once you're in Settings, find "Language & Region" — it's usually listed right
below "Date & Time." Tap into it, change the region to "United States," and save.

![Find Language & Region in Settings and go in to change the region](https://shadowrocket.ink/img/id-10002.jpg)

This step is the key one: only after the region is changed will the Apple account
sign-in entry at the top of Settings follow the US registration flow. If you skip
this and go straight to registering, the resulting account will still carry your
original region, which defeats the purpose.

## Step 3: Enter the Apple account registration entry

After changing the region, go back to the very top of Settings and tap into the
Apple account sign-in entry. If this device isn't signed into any Apple account
yet, you'll see a line reading "Don't have an Apple Account?" — tap it to start
the new-account registration flow.

![Tap "Don't have an Apple Account?" to start registration](https://shadowrocket.ink/img/id-10003.jpg)

## Step 4: Fill in an email address

Next you'll be asked to enter an email address, which will serve as the sign-in
email for the new Apple account. If you don't have a suitable email on hand, you
can also tap "Don't have an email address?" below to use one of Apple's alternative
options. The "Notifications" toggle under the email field is on by default, meaning
you agree to receive email from Apple; turn it off if you don't want that — it
doesn't affect the registration process.

![Enter an email address to register the new Apple account](https://shadowrocket.ink/img/id-10004.jpg)

## Step 5: Verify the email address

After filling in your name, email, and other basic information, the system will
prompt you to "Verify Your Email Address" and send a verification email to the
address you entered. Until this step is complete, the account is technically
created but you won't be able to use services like iCloud or the App Store, so
don't forget to check your inbox and click the confirmation link to verify.

![Verify the email address to activate the account](https://shadowrocket.ink/img/id-10005.jpg)

Once verification is complete, you'll have a brand-new Apple account with the
region set to the United States. Sign into the App Store with it and you'll see
the US-exclusive app catalog. Keep in mind that this new account is completely
separate from your usual Apple ID — contacts, photos, and App Store purchase
history won't sync between them, so pay attention to which account you're signed
into to avoid mixing up data or purchases.
